## Slide 3 — 507 & 510: AI Governance and AI Safety Kick-off (Key Objectives)

*Key Objectives | Two parallel workstreams | 8 weeks | 4 sprints*

### 507 — AI Governance and Value Realization Framework
- Identify and prioritize high value AI opportunities aligned to the enterprise objectives.
- Inventory AI already in use, including vendor supplied features and unsanctioned adoption.
- Define risk tiering, the policy suite and a single intake for all AI demand.
- Establish AI governance on the NIST AI Risk Management Framework and Vermont Bulletin 229.
- Establish the value realization model and a board accountable governance operating model.

### 510 — AI Safety, Security, Privacy and Governance Framework
- Make AI safety, security, privacy and compliance controls integral to the platform by design.
- Define the control scope by AI usage pattern against published control sets.
- Design data protection, identity scoped access and output assurance for regulated data.
- Establish human oversight evidence for individualized determinations on care decisions.
- Design monitoring, incident response and the readiness criteria that decide go live.

> **Callout:** Governance and assurance developed in tandem, so the approval path and the control set operate as one system.

## Slide 4 — 507 & 510: AI Governance and AI Safety Kick-off (Outcomes)

### 507 — AI Governance and Value Realization Framework
- **Value Identification:** A prioritized AI portfolio mapped to affordability, experience and growth.
- **Measurable Benefit:** A value model with named benefit owners, so value is evidenced not asserted.
- **Regulatory Readiness:** The inventory, risk classification and evidence pack an examiner can review.
- **Governed Adoption:** One intake and one policy suite, so the governed route is the fastest route.

### 510 — AI Safety, Security, Privacy and Governance Framework
- **Safety by Design:** Controls embedded as design constraints, not raised as later findings.
- **Clinical Defensibility:** Evidence demonstrating an individualized determination on care decisions.
- **Operational Assurance:** Monitoring and readiness criteria that make AI behavior controllable.
- **Regulatory Compliance:** Alignment to the NIST profile, Bulletin 229 and the Section 1557 duty.

## Slide 9 — Governance rigor scales proportionately with the calculated risk profile *(Illustrative)*

| Tier | Risk Profile | Governance Rigor |
|---|---|---|
| **Tier 1: Critical/High-Risk** | Direct patient impact, clinical/regulated decisions, PHI processing. | Exec Committee approval, independent validation, DPIA, continuous monitoring. |
| **Tier 2: Moderate-Risk** | Operational decisions, administrative automation, non-PHI processing. | Governance Council approval, standard security review, periodic recertification. |
| **Tier 3: Low-Risk** | Internal productivity, no consumer impact, experimental/sandbox. | Technical Review Board approval, basic monitoring. |

**Risk Scoring Model:** (Impact × Likelihood) + (Regulatory Exposure × Detection Difficulty)

## Slide 13 — Track & Evaluate Clinical Utilization – Prior Authorization use case (current state) *(Illustrative)*

Swimlane process diagram. Lanes: **Customer**, **Provider**, **Specialist**, **Payer**.

Flow:
1. **Provider:** Submit prior-authorization for patient treatment / prescription →
2. **Payer:** Receive request and verify documents →
3. **Payer:** Clinical team checks eligibility with benefits plan, claim / medical history →
4. **Payer:** If determination not made, case is escalated for further clinical review →
5. **Payer:** Make determination and issue decision
   - **Rejected** (red arrow) → **Customer:** Receive rejection letter with summary of benefits and instructions for appeal
   - **Approved** (green arrow) → **Customer:** Receive treatment / prescription →
6. **Payer:** Clinical team monitors treatment and conducts post-care review (follows from received treatment)

## Slide 14 — Track & Evaluate Clinical Utilization – Prior Authorization use case (AI-enabled future state) *(Illustrative)*

Swimlane process diagram (AI-agent steps shown as dashed boxes). Lanes: **Customer**, **Provider**, **Specialist**, **Payer**.

Flow:
1. **Provider:** AI Agent within EHR system determines if prior authorization for treatment is needed, and submits →
2. **Payer:** AI Agent analyzes file, medical codes, non-standard narrative text against policy and benefits plan →
3. **Payer:** For cases that closely match benefit rules, AI Agent approves — **Approved** (green arrow) → **Customer:** Receive treatment/prescription
4. **Payer:** Flag highly complex or likely-to-deny cases for Human in the Loop →
5. **Payer:** Escalated to clinical team to make final determination and issue decision
   - **Approved** (green arrow) → **Customer:** Receive treatment/prescription
   - **Rejected** (red arrow) → **Customer:** AI agent generates plain-language denial letters citing exact clinical criteria and specific missing requirements
6. **Payer:** AI agent monitors EHR feeds to predict discharge readiness dates and suggest post-acute placements (post-treatment)
7. **Payer:** AI agent cross checks bill codes with physician documentation to flag discrepancies

## Slide 22 — 510 | Sprint 1 - Define the Control Scope and Baseline

*Establish which controls apply to which AI usage patterns, against published control sets rather than authored ones.*

### Inputs
- The AI inventory from workstream 507
- Current security architecture, topology and identity design
- Data classification scheme and current handling standards
- Current logging, monitoring and security event coverage
- Vendor security documentation for platforms with AI features
- Written confirmation that no production access is in scope

### Activities
- Confirm scope, deliverables, acceptance criteria and the boundary
- Define the AI usage patterns in scope, from search to agentic
- Confirm the Cloud Security Alliance matrix and OWASP lists as sources
- Assess current controls against the control set for each pattern
- Agree the shared control vocabulary with workstream 507

### Deliverables
- Agreed scope, deliverables and acceptance criteria
- AI usage patterns defined with the control set applying to each
- Baseline gap assessment by pattern with a named owner
- Shared control vocabulary agreed with workstream 507
- Scope boundary confirmed in writing

## Slide 25 — AI Governance vs. AI Safety, Security, and Privacy *(appendix)*

### AI Governance Framework — What must be Governed?
- Who is accountable?
- What requirements apply?
- What risk tier applies?
- Who approves?
- What gates must be passed?
- How AI is monitored?
- How is Assurance demonstrated?

### AI Safety, Security, & Privacy Framework — What must be Controlled?
- What safeguards are required?
- How are requirements implemented?
- What controls does each tier require?
- What evidence is required?
- How is readiness demonstrated?
- What signals and thresholds are monitored?
- What evidence demonstrates control effectiveness?
